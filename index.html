About

Established in 1927, the Florida Theater is a historical landmark in downtown Jacksonville, Fl that continues to operate as a performing arts center. The 1,865 seat theatre is ran by a nonprofit organization in a partnership with the City of Jacksonville, who owns the building. It is known for its historical significance and for being a broadcast host for many national radio and TV programs.

Visit their website: floridatheatre.com

History

The Florida Theatre started its construction in the summer of 1926 on land previously occupied by a police station & jail. The structure was seven stories tall, and even featured a roof garden. After construction was complete, the theatre opened to the public on April 8, 1927 as a place to show movies and live performances. The theatre presented several programs on opening day, including an 18-piece ensemble called Frank Morris and the Brilliant Florida Orchestra, as well as the film Let It Rain. The theatre continued to show a variety of content through the years, but it was on a financial decline during the 1970s and on May 8, 1980, it was forced to close. Its historical significance, though, led to a $500,000 dollar grant from the State of Florida and a $350,000 grant from the City of Jacksonville. It was later purchased and renovated by the Arts Assembly of Jacksonville for a collective cost of about $6 million. On August 26, 1983, one year after the purchase, the Florida Theatre was once again opened to the public. Today, the Florida Theater houses showings for the Florida Ballet, Theatreworks, and annual Community Nutcracker.

Famous For

The Florida Theatre is not only well known for its historical value, it also is famous for specific moments involving its past performers. One particularly well known controversy involves Elvis Presley, where his performances from the Florida Theatre caused concern among city leaders of Jacksonville. When Elvis returned the next year, a committee had formed, and Juvenile Court Judge Marion Gooding had prepared arrest warrents against him. Judge Gooding invited Elvis into his office, and threatened to execute the warrents if Elvis didn't tone down his performance for the teenage audience. The show was performed, and no action was taken from the police or the judge.


Additionally, the Florida Theatre is also knows as the place that Derek Trucks first played with the Allman Brothers Band at eleven years old in 1991. That night, they played the song One Way Out.
